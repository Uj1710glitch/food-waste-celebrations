Where a celebration's food goes

Food service and household events are where waste is easiest to prevent, because the surplus is planned in.

Over-ordering

Hosts cook for the biggest possible guest list and add extra "to be safe", so surplus is built in before the first guest arrives.

The buffet effect

Large platters and big plates encourage people to take more than they eat. Untouched food in open buffets often cannot be reused.

No plan for leftovers

Without containers, a donation contact or a pickup arrangement, safe food is thrown out at the end of the night.

Landfill

Buried food rots without oxygen and releases methane, a potent greenhouse gas, along with liquid runoff that can pollute soil and water.

Long-term impact on the planet

Hover over the cards. The damage compounds: lost resources, then emissions, then pressure on people.

🌾

Land

Farmland, often cleared from forest, is used to grow food nobody eats. Soil is depleted and habitats are lost for nothing.

About a quarter or more of farmland feeds waste
💧

Water

Irrigation is the largest use of freshwater. Wasted food means that water, plus the water used in cooking, was spent in vain.

Meat and dairy waste cost the most water
🔥

Climate

Emissions occur at every step, then landfill adds methane. Over decades this speeds up warming and weather extremes.

Methane traps far more heat than CO₂ in the short term
🍽️

People

Surplus that could be rescued sits in bins while millions go hungry, and food prices feel the strain of lost supply.

Waste and hunger exist side by side

Plan the party, not the waste

Small decisions before the event decide how much is left after it.

Count guests honestlyUse RSVPs and plan for actual attendance, not the maximum.
Serve smaller, refill oftenSmaller plates and staged serving cut over-taking.
Arrange leftovers earlyLine up a food bank, shelter or rescue group before the event.
Offer take-home boxesGuests happily carry food home when containers are ready.
Compost the restInedible scraps become soil instead of methane.
Cook seasonal and localLower transport emissions and less spoilage.

Illustrative estimate only: assumes ~0.5 kg of food per guest and a 20% cut in surplus through the steps above.
